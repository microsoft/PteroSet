## 2. New load-bearing gaps

### 2.1 Finding 1 — The "clean-single-only" comparability arm has an unaddressed, likely severe selection-bias problem

**Why this matters.** The consensus's secondary arm is not "single-label via largest-overlap-wins arbitration" (what I proposed in Round 2, §7.2) — it is *restriction to windows that already contain exactly one distinct species*, with no arbitration needed. That is a cleaner, more honest construction than my Round 2 proposal (it never manufactures a single label for a genuinely multi-species window), but it introduces a different, unaddressed problem: **this filtered subset is not a random or representative sample of the species-positive population.**

Species that tend to vocalize in acoustically crowded conditions (dawn chorus peaks, multi-species mixed-species flocks, high-density habitat/season) will be systematically under-represented in the clean-single subset relative to their true prevalence, while species that tend to call in isolation (off-peak hours, low-density habitats, distinctive solitary/loud calls that annotators mark without competing detections) will be over-represented. This is a **selection effect correlated with the very acoustic/ecological covariates the classifier is trying to learn from**, not a random subsampling. Two concrete consequences:

1. Any metric computed on the clean-single arm (macro-F1, per-class F1, confusion matrix) describes performance on a **systematically different, unstated population** than the species-positive population as a whole — it is not simply "the same task on less data," it is a different task with a shifted class-conditional distribution.
2. Under PteroSet's LOPO design, the *degree* of this selection effect can vary by held-out project (a project with denser/richer soundscapes will lose a larger fraction of its species-positive windows to the co-occurrence filter than a sparser one), meaning the clean-single arm's per-fold sample sizes and class balance are not comparable across folds even before considering eligibility (item 6). A fold could look artificially "easier" or "harder" in the comparability arm purely because that project's chorus density differs, not because the species discrimination task itself differs.

**What is missing from all three Round 2 drafts and the consensus list.** Nowhere is it specified that the clean-single arm's *representativeness* must itself be measured and disclosed. Without that, a reader could mistake "Perch+LogReg gets macro-F1 0.89 on the Orcas-comparable single-label arm" for a general statement about species-ID quality, when it may only describe performance on the acoustically-simplest, most-isolated-call subset of the data.

**Concrete fix, minimal and cheap** (pure bookkeeping on already-computed label tables, no new modeling):
- For every species, report `clean_single_prevalence_ratio = P(species | window is in clean-single subset) / P(species | window is species-positive)`. A ratio far from 1.0 in either direction is disclosed per-species alongside every clean-single-arm metric.
- Report the clean-single subset's retention rate **per project** (not just pooled), since LOPO folds inherit whichever project is held out — this is a one-line addition to the existing per-fold reporting table (item 6) any of the three drafts already build.
- Add an explicit, permanent caveat wherever the clean-single arm's numbers are reported: *"comparability-with-orcas_dclde2026 methodology only; not a representative-population estimate of species-ID quality — see macro-AP on the full multilabel population (item 1) for that claim."* This should be a literal sentence in the eventual results report template (Phase 4/Documenter handoff in all three drafts), not just a design-doc footnote that can be lost in translation.

This does not change any pipeline code, storage format, or milestone — it is a required reporting discipline that closes a real interpretability/validity gap in an otherwise sound design.

### 2.2 Finding 2 — Cross-fold macro-AP/macro-F1 is not well-defined when the eligible-species set varies by fold

**Why this matters.** Item 6 (per-fold eligibility masks) is correctly identified as mandatory by the consensus, and item 7 (macro-AP primary) is correctly identified as the headline metric. But nobody in Round 2 worked through what happens when these two are combined across PteroSet's 5 LOPO folds: **the set of Gate-B-eligible species differs, potentially substantially, from fold to fold**, because eligibility (§6.2, my Round 2) is defined per fold from that fold's own train/test support. A species scarce enough to be ineligible in fold 0 (MAP1 held out) may be comfortably eligible in fold 2 (PPA2 held out), and vice versa.

If macro-AP is computed per fold over "that fold's own eligible species" (the natural, already-specified behavior), then **fold 0's macro-AP and fold 2's macro-AP are averages over two different, non-nested sets of classes** — they are not two independent measurements of the same quantity, and reporting `mean(macro_AP across 5 folds) ± std` as "the" cross-validated performance number, the way both my Round 2 §8.3 and the pipeline's `fulldata_results_species_cv_summary.csv` (§8, §11 Phase 3) describe doing, silently answers a different, unstated question: *"how good is the model, on average, at whatever species happen to be testable in each held-out project"* — not *"how good is the model at species X, generalized across projects."* Both questions are legitimate, but they are not the same question, and none of the three Round 2 drafts distinguishes them in the metric definition itself (my Round 2 came closest, noting per-species fold-eligibility-count as an annotation, §8.3, but did not resolve what the *scalar* cross-fold summary should mean).

**Concrete fix:**
- Define and report **two** distinct cross-fold scalars, not one, both cheap to compute from artifacts the consensus already produces:
  1. **`macro_AP_core`**: macro-AP restricted to the subset of species eligible in **all 5 folds** (a fixed, common denominator) — this is the only version of "mean macro-AP across folds" that is a statistically valid repeated measurement of the same quantity, and should be the one headline cross-validation number.
  2. **`macro_AP_per_fold`**: the already-specified per-fold macro-AP over that fold's own eligible set, reported as 5 separate numbers (not averaged into one scalar), each annotated with its eligible-species count and list — useful for understanding project-specific behavior, but explicitly not to be presented as "the" cross-validated score.
- This requires no new modeling and no new artifact beyond what item 6 already produces — it is a metric-definition fix (which subset each aggregate is computed over) that must be written into `train_perch_logreg.py`/`evaluate_linear_probe.py`'s metric-aggregation code (whichever of the three drafts' script names is adopted) before Phase 3, not discovered after a confusing headline number is already reported.

### 2.3 Finding 3 — The composite-identity/source-hash mechanism protects against the wrong half of the repo's own documented failure mode

**Why this matters.** Item 11 is explicitly motivated, in the pipeline Round 2 draft (§6), by *this repository's own documented history*: a row-count-equal embedding/label cache silently served stale content after an upstream annotation change, causing a real, already-observed v2→v3 PPA4 regression. The proposed mitigation (mine: git commit + input file SHA-256; pipeline: a single `source_windows_json_sha256` field checked against `windows_mapping_4.0overlap_segmented_v4.json`) hashes the **window geometry** file. That is the correct defense against a *window-geometry* change silently invalidating a stale cache — but it is not the artifact most likely to change during this specific project's own near-term work.

Per Round 1's dataset audit (carried into every subsequent round unchanged): `species.csv` has known duplicate codes (`RAMTUC`/`RHATUC`, `ATRPIL`/`ATAPIL`) and rank-mixed placeholders (`PSITTACIDAE`, `TYRANN_SP1`, etc.) that are explicitly expected to be cleaned up via a `species_taxonomy_crosswalk.csv` during Phase 0/1 — i.e., **the species-label-derivation chain (`species.csv`, the crosswalk, `annotations_species.json`, and the label-schema/`min_class_support`/`OTHER`-bucket configuration) is the artifact most likely to be revised iteratively, multiple times, during exactly the phase this caching system needs to protect.** Under the pipeline draft's own design (§5, `build_fold_embeddings.py` bakes `species_multihot` directly into each fold's materialized `.npz`, joined from `species_labels_path` + `class_list_path`), a crosswalk fix that corrects the RAMTUC/RHATUC duplicate changes the multi-hot label matrix without changing a single byte of `windows_mapping_4.0overlap_segmented_v4.json` or the embeddings themselves. If the cache-invalidation hash covers only the windows-mapping file (as currently specified), a re-run after a crosswalk fix will pass the row-count check, pass the geometry-hash check, and **silently serve fold `.npz` files containing stale, pre-fix species labels joined onto correctly-fresh embeddings** — reproducing the exact failure class the mechanism was built to prevent, in the one place it is most likely to actually occur on this project.

**Concrete fix**, scoped as narrowly as the pipeline draft's own "one targeted hash" philosophy (§6 explicitly rejects a full per-window manifest system in favor of a single scalar check — this fix keeps that same minimalism, it only changes what the scalar covers):
- Compute the stored provenance hash over the **concatenation** of: `windows_mapping_4.0overlap_segmented_v4.json` SHA-256, `species.csv` SHA-256, `species_taxonomy_crosswalk.csv` SHA-256 (once that artifact exists), `annotations_species.json` SHA-256, and a version string for the label-schema logic itself (six-state schema version / `min_class_support` value / `OTHER`-bucket policy — anything that changes the label derivation without changing any input file's bytes, e.g. a `min_class_support` config edit).
- Store this as one combined hash field (e.g. `label_and_geometry_provenance_sha256`) on **both** the pool `.npz` (embeddings — technically only needs the geometry component, but storing the full combined hash is one field and avoids a second, differently-scoped hash to keep track of) and, more importantly, on **every per-fold materialized `.npz`** that bakes labels in — the per-fold artifact is where this bug actually bites, and it is currently the one place none of the three Round 2 drafts proposes a hash check at all (pipeline's §6 hash lives only on the pool file).
- This is a small, mechanical addition (one more file read + hash concatenation, already-cheap per pipeline's own cost accounting) that directly closes a gap the project has already paid for once in a different but structurally identical form.

### 2.4 Finding 4 — "No silence fallback" needs an explicit replacement behavior, and it must be sequenced before eligibility-mask computation

**Why this matters.** The consensus rejects the sibling script's "substitute silence + warn and continue" behavior on load failure (correctly — a zero-valued embedding is a well-defined "no signal" case for the binary bird/no-bird task but an ambiguous one for a multilabel species head, as I argued in Round 2 §5). But "no silence fallback" alone specifies only what *not* to do, not what happens instead, and the two most natural replacements have materially different consequences that none of the three drafts distinguishes:

- **Hard-fail the whole extraction run** on any load failure — safest against silent data corruption, but means a single corrupt/missing audio file (which, given `data/audios_48khz/`'s history of path-resolution quirks already found in Round 1, is not a zero-probability event) halts a multi-hour GPU extraction pass entirely.
- **Exclude the specific window and log it** — keeps the run resilient, but **shrinks the effective, embeddable window pool** relative to whatever population the species-label CSVs (built in an earlier phase, per all three drafts' phase ordering) were computed against.

The second option is almost certainly the right operational choice, but it creates a concrete ordering dependency the consensus list does not surface: **if per-fold support counts (item 6's eligibility masks) are computed from the label CSVs before extraction, and extraction then silently excludes some fraction of windows for I/O reasons, the eligibility masks are computed against a support count that no longer matches the actual set of windows that will have embeddings.** A species that was marginally eligible (support exactly at `min_support`) could have its true, post-extraction support drop below threshold without the eligibility computation ever re-running.

**Concrete fix:**
- Adopt "exclude and log" as the specified behavior (not silently implied by "no silence fallback"), with every excluded `window_id` written to a small, versioned exclusions manifest (`reports/species_extraction_exclusions.csv`), mirroring the discipline pipeline's own hard-gate philosophy already applies elsewhere (§5/§6: "abort with a clear error naming the offending window_id — never a silent drop" — excluding-and-logging is the multilabel-appropriate analogue of that same discipline, not a relaxation of it).
- Enforce, as a Phase 1/2 acceptance gate (already the right place per all three drafts' phased structure), that eligibility-mask computation (item 6) runs **after** the extraction-exclusions manifest is finalized, consuming the post-exclusion support counts — not the pre-extraction label-CSV support counts. This is a one-step reordering in the phase plan already specified in Round 2, not new machinery.
- Set an explicit, small acceptance bound on the exclusion rate itself (`[RECOMMENDATION]` <0.1% of windows, consistent with my Round 2 §15 Phase 2 acceptance criterion) — above that, treat it as a data-pipeline bug to fix, not a rate to silently tolerate and route around via exclusion.

### 2.5 Finding 5 — No convergence/near-separability diagnostic is specified for the mandatory per-class OvR fits

**Why this matters.** Item 5 (fixed `C=1.0`, `solver="lbfgs"`, `max_iter=1000`, no grid search) is a reasonable, evidence-backed default for a first pass (minimalist §9.1's determinism argument is sound). But this exact hyperparameter set is now applied, per item 1, to a **mandatory OvR decomposition with K independent per-class binary fits over a long-tailed, severely imbalanced species list** (Round 1's own curation already anticipates classes near a `min_support` floor of ~10). A 1536-dimensional embedding space with a rare class's positive count in the low tens is a textbook setting for **perfect or near-perfect linear separability by chance alone** (high dimensionality relative to sample size), which for unregularized-enough logistic regression (`C=1.0` is fairly weak regularization) produces one of two silent failure modes: LBFGS hits `max_iter=1000` without converging (a `ConvergenceWarning` that none of the three Round 2 drafts proposes capturing or gating on), or it does converge but to a numerically unstable, badly-overconfident decision boundary (very large coefficient norms, probabilities saturated near 0/1) that inflates or deflates the very AP metric (item 7) that is supposed to be the trustworthy headline number for exactly these rare, highest-uncertainty species.

None of the three Round 2 drafts specifies checking `clf.estimators_[i].n_iter_ < max_iter` or capturing `sklearn.exceptions.ConvergenceWarning` per class, per fold — the closest is minimalist's §15 risk note that `class_weight="balanced"` "is a one-argument change if Phase 2's results show it is needed," which treats this as a possible *performance* tuning question, not a *numerical validity* gate that should run automatically and unconditionally.

**Concrete fix**, cheap and mechanical (a check on an object `sklearn` already returns, no new modeling):
- For every per-class fit in the OvR decomposition, per fold, record `n_iter_` and whether a `ConvergenceWarning` was raised (`warnings.catch_warnings(record=True)` around each `.fit()` call).
- Add a **numerical-trust flag**, orthogonal to and in addition to item 6's support-based eligibility mask: a class/fold combination that failed to converge is marked `numerically_untrusted` and excluded from `macro_AP_core`/`macro_AP_per_fold` (Finding 2's two scalars) exactly as an ineligible class would be, with its raw score still reported in the full per-species table but visually/programmatically flagged, never silently averaged in alongside converged fits.
- This closes a specific, testable gap without adding a hyperparameter search — it is a diagnostic on the existing mandatory recipe, consistent with the "fixed C=1, no grid search" decision in item 5 rather than contradicting it.

## 4. Verdict on the "arbitrary co-occurrence headline threshold" question

The consensus's move away from a single pre-registered co-occurrence-rate threshold (my Round 2 §6.1's 5%, minimalist's §7 90%/10% pair) toward *unconditionally* running both the mandatory multilabel arm and the clean-single-only comparability arm is a **genuine improvement in scientific defensibility**: it removes an arbitrary, hard-to-justify numeric cutoff from the critical path of deciding what the primary scientific claim is, and instead lets the measured co-occurrence rate stand as a reported descriptive statistic (context for interpreting the clean-single arm's representativeness, per Finding 1) rather than a branch-selection gate whose exact threshold value has no principled derivation. **This part of the consensus is scientifically complete as stated and should not be revisited or re-litigated with a new threshold.**

However, "no arbitrary threshold in the branch-selection decision" is not the same claim as "the metric-aggregation and caching design built around that decision is complete." Findings 1–5 are gaps in the latter, not attempts to reopen the former.

## 5. Overall verdict

**NEEDS-MORE.**

The twelve-item consensus is architecturally sound and should not be redesigned or restarted. Nine of the twelve items are fully resolved by the existing Round 2 work with no new issue found (§1). Three items (2, 4, 11) surface genuinely new, load-bearing scientific/correctness gaps not identified by any of the three Round 2 drafts individually, and one additional cross-cutting consequence of already-agreed items 1+6+7 (Finding 2) and items 1+5 (Finding 5) was found by combining decisions that were each individually fine in isolation. None of the five findings requires reopening the label-provenance design, the leakage/fold-reuse policy, the extract-once-pool architecture, the dependency/environment decisions, or the window/segment/file-granularity decision — all of those are confirmed converged. What is required before Phase 0 code is written and trusted:

1. Add the clean-single-arm representativeness diagnostic and permanent non-generalizability caveat (§2.1).
2. Define `macro_AP_core` (fixed common eligible-species set) as the sole cross-fold headline scalar, distinct from the five per-fold `macro_AP_per_fold` numbers (§2.2).
3. Extend the composite provenance hash to cover the species-label-derivation chain, not just window geometry, and attach it to the per-fold materialized caches specifically, not only the pool file (§2.3).
4. Specify "exclude and log" (not hard-fail) as the no-silence-fallback replacement behavior, and sequence eligibility-mask computation strictly after the extraction-exclusions manifest is finalized (§2.4).
5. Add a per-class, per-fold convergence/near-separability diagnostic as a numerical-trust flag orthogonal to the support-based eligibility mask (§2.5).

All five are small, mechanical additions to already-planned artifacts and phase boundaries — none adds a new phase, a new dependency, or a new modeling choice. This is a patch pass on a converged design, not a fourth round of architectural rework.
